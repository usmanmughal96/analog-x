Method, quantitative checks and evidence limitations

Global search: the engine enumerates the provider's public one-degree GLO-90 land-tile inventory, partitions it into 24 longitude shards, reads each COG and resamples to a nominal 925 m local metric grid. It then evaluates non-overlapping 6×6-cell windows. The merge report provides assigned, processed, failed and no-window tile counts; incomplete or failed shards prevent a complete-scan status.

Two terrain metric families: the core index uses median slope, P90 slope and P90 local relief. The secondary diagnostics use P95 slope, elevation IQR and detrended roughness. Both compare window descriptors with Moon/Mars reference-window medians using IQR scales from the ten-site Earth reference pack. Their agreement is a robustness check within the same DEM, not independent-source validation or calibrated accuracy.

Independent DEM check: a separate NASADEM/SRTM-derived cross-check is attempted for selected sites within its approximate 56°S–60°N coverage. Copernicus DSM and NASADEM elevation products differ in acquisition and surface semantics. Reported descriptor differences/rank correlations are diagnostic only and are not ground truth.

Field validation: no physical field observations are created by this engine. Pakistan sites remain desk-evidence leads until georeferenced photographs, calibrated instrument readings, sample provenance, permissions and independent review are recorded in the field evidence ledger.

Do not over-interpret: Copernicus GLO-90 is a digital surface model. Terrain descriptors do not measure lithology, mineralogy, soil strength, regolith, dust, ice, resources, radiation, illumination, gravity, rover traversability, landing safety or base suitability. The global shortlist has a bounded number of results per target and is not an exhaustive export of every candidate window.
